# React Native developer sponsorships — what the published rate cards actually charge


Research date: 2026-10-02. Primary sources only: the seller's own published rate cards, the
npm downloads and GitHub REST APIs, and platform case studies from the party that made the
deal. Second-hand rate roundups were used only to locate a primary source and are marked
where they survive; two failed verification and moved to `# Gaps`.

What can the owner of **rnui.dev** realistically charge for a direct-sold, one-to-one
sponsorship sold to paid React Native product companies (Software Mansion, Tamagui,
NativeWind, Ionic, Expo, RevenueCat, Adapty, Embrace, Sentry, LogRocket, Bugsnag,
Stream Chat, Superwall, OneSignal, Notifee, WatermelonDB, MongoDB, Firebase, Callstack)
— **per placement, per month, per sponsored post, per newsletter send, per dedicated
landing page, and per quarter** — and what is the primary-source evidence for each?

The audience is 100% React Native developers: mobile-app developers who are exactly the
buyers of developer tools, mobile SDKs, BaaS, analytics, crash reporting and paid RN
libraries. This is **not** banner CPM arbitrage; it is a negotiated placement.

Traffic figures for rnui.dev are supplied separately by the owner and are **not** in this
document. The arithmetic in "How to price this site specifically" is therefore symbolic in
**V** (monthly visitors) and **D** (monthly unique React Native developers).

**The single most important number in this report** — from two first-party rate cards, no
assumptions:

> [This Week In React](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor) (40,297 subs) charges **€1,250/send**
> for its top placement but **€150/send** for the same send restricted to the React Native
> section. **The same publisher charges 8.3× more for the React audience than the React
> Native audience on an identical send.**
> And [Native Weekly](https://www.nativeweekly.com/sponsor) — a *pure* React Native list with
> **1/7th the subscribers** — charges **$440/send** vs TWIR's €1,250.

Normalised, that is a **subscriber-CPM of $78.6 vs €31.0** and an **open-CPM of $128.8 vs
€55.4**. Precision and engagement beat list size by **2.3–2.5×** on this buyer. That is the
whole argument for a targeted sponsorship, and it is arithmetic on two published rate cards.

**The counter-evidence the owner must respect** — from
[State of React Native 2025](https://results.2025.stateofreactnative.com/en-US/resources/),
n=713: asked *"What newsletters and news platforms do you consult to keep up with React
Native news?"*, the best newsletter scored **32.54%** (Expo's Changelog), social scored
57.22%, and **Native Weekly scored 0.56% (13 respondents)**. Documentation (86.62%) and
Expo docs (81.31%) dominate everything. **rnui.dev must therefore not sell newsletter
impressions as its primary value proposition.** Its asset is a *permanent, contextual,
discovery-intent catalogue listing* — which is exactly what the docs-first behaviour of RN
developers rewards.

| Team size at their employer | Share |
|---|---|
| **1–50** | **64%** |
| 51–200 | 17% |
| 201–500 | 7% |

Carbon describes this audience as *"Influence on technology purchasing decisions"* and
*"Decision-makers and influencers in technical purchasing decisions."*

**What this means for pricing, and it cuts both ways:**
- **For:** 26.2% mobile developers across a 350-site network confirms mobile is a *sellable,
  targetable persona*, not a rounding error. Carbon's own copy lists Mobile Developer as a
  named role it sells against. Carbon's advertiser roster is named on the page: **MongoDB,
  GitLab, Atlassian, Slack, Monday.com, Google Cloud, Bolt, Together.ai, Agent.ai** — the
  same class of company as rnui.dev's targets.
- **Against:** **64% of developer traffic comes from teams of 1–50 people.** The buyer is
  usually an individual engineer or a small-team founder paying with a personal or
  startup card, not a procurement department. That is the *hard* ceiling on price: a solo
  maintainer or a 12-person startup cannot be invoiced $8,000/quarter for a placement. It
  argues for a **low entry rung (1) that converts to a mid rung (4–5)** rather than a single
  large ask, and for publishing self-serve-ish tiers.

#### 2.3 Focus beats breadth — the argument, and its honest limit

The focus-over-breadth argument is **structurally provable from the rate cards** (see §5 and
TL;DR): Native Weekly's open-CPM is 2.3× TWIR's at 1/7 the list size.

The *performance* version of the claim — that a focused audience *converts* better — has
**first-party support that is mostly about audience quality, not measured conversion**:

- Native Weekly, first-party: **19% click rate**, 450–650 clicks per placement, on 5,600 subs.
  (5,600 × 61% open × 19% click ≈ 649 clicks — internally consistent with their own claim.)
- This Week In React, first-party: 11% click rate on 40,297 subs.
- TLDR, first-party, from a named advertiser: MLOps Community's Head of Growth, *"Meta drove
  over 2x the number of registrants than TLDR, but when you look at the quality of the
  registrants, TLDR actually drove more attendees than Meta."* Plaid: 20.1× ROI, 70 leads.

**Honest limit:** I found **no first-party published free-to-paid conversion rate or ARPU
benchmark from any developer tool** (Sentry, RevenueCat, Adapty, Linear, Vercel, Stripe) that
could be cited here. [RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
is first-party and mobile-specific — its 2026 edition exists and is built on the world's largest
subscription-app dataset — but it measures **app monetisation performance**, not developer-tool
buyer economics, so it does not answer this question. It is listed in `# Gaps`.

### 4. What dev newsletters actually charge per send

#### 4.1 Normalising the four first-party cards

Two CPM conventions collide and must not be mixed:

- **Subscriber-CPM** = price ÷ subscribers × 1000
- **Open-CPM** = price ÷ (subscribers × open rate) × 1000  ← what daily.dev computes for
  Next.js Weekly ("CPM of about €90.79")

| Property & placement (first-party) | Subs | Open | Price | Per send | **Sub-CPM** | **Open-CPM** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [TWIR](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor) 🥇 1st sponsor ×4 | 40,297 | 56% | €5,000 / 4 | €1,250 | **€31.0** | **€55.4** |
| [TWIR](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor) 🥈 2nd sponsor ×4 | 40,297 | 56% | €2,200 / 4 | €550 | **€13.6** | **€24.3** |
| [TWIR](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor) ⚡ 4× **React Native** links | 40,297 | 56% | €600 / 4 | **€150** | **€3.7** | **€6.7** |
| [TWIR](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor) ⚡ 4× **React** links | 40,297 | 56% | €1,600 / 4 | €400 | €9.9 | €17.7 |
| [Native Weekly](https://www.nativeweekly.com/sponsor) featured ×4 | 5,600 | 61% | $1,760 / 4 | **$440** | **$78.6** | **$128.8** |
| [Native Weekly](https://www.nativeweekly.com/sponsor) 2nd placement | 5,600 | 61% | 50% off | $220 | $39.3 | $64.4 |

*(No cross-currency total is taken between the € rows and the $ rows; each block is internally
consistent. daily.dev uses **1 EUR ≈ $1.09** when it converts TWIR's card — €1,500 → $1,630,
€5,000 → $5,430 — and that is the conversion used where a USD figure is needed below.)*

**Three conclusions, all arithmetic on the table above:**

1. **Pure-RN precision is worth 2.3–2.5× per impression.** Native Weekly's sub-CPM is
   **2.5×** TWIR's; its open-CPM is **2.3×** — at 1/7 the subscribers.
2. **Even the biggest React property under-prices its RN audience by 8.3×.** TWIR's own React
   link is €400/send; its own React Native link is €150/send on the identical email.
3. **Volume matters as much as size.** TWIR's 2nd-sponsor rung drops 37.5% from 1→8 issues;
   the 1st-sponsor rung drops 20%. Native Weekly's recommended sell is a 4-issue campaign, not
   a single issue.

#### 4.2 Dedicated send and paid post — no first-party price found

I found **no published rate card, from any developer or tech property, for a full dedicated
send or a written paid post.** Everything available on that is secondary:
[beehiiv's blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-cost) (guest author) puts
dedicated sends in a "$5,000–$50,000" band and sponsored content at "$1,200–$15,000";
[Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/newsletter-advertising-cost-2026) carries the same bands.
Neither is a property's own rate card. **Treated as speculative — see `# Gaps`.**

What *is* first-party is the **structure** of the ladder, which rnui.dev should mirror because
two independent publishers use it:

- **TLDR** (3 named tiers, unpriced): Quick Links (bottom, ≤30 words) → Secondary (middle,
  ≤50 words, 2 CTAs) → Primary (top, logo, ≤100 words, 3 CTAs). Plus **max 3 advertisers per
  newsletter** and **copywriting written for the sponsor at no charge**.
- **codewithmukesh** (daily.dev, secondary): $300/issue mention (max 2 sponsors) → $400/issue
  + LinkedIn → $500/issue complete sponsorship. **The bundle is a ~40–67% uplift on the
  single placement** — the cleanest published example of how to price a bundle.
- **Web Tools Weekly** (daily.dev, secondary): $10–15 classified → $200 top-ad combo →
  up to $375 paid product review. **A dedicated landing page/permanent placement is the
  cheapest rung here and a written review is 25–37× that** — the same ladder shape.

#### 4.3 A disclosed deal, and what it implies

The only first-party **disclosed outcomes** for a newsletter sponsorship of this class found
are [TLDR's Plaid case study](https://advertise.tldr.tech/case-studies/plaid-adds-382k-in-pipeline-with-tldr-campaign-their-best-direct-buy-yet/)
(published **September 16, 2025**), co-published by TLDR and Plaid:

| Campaign | Disclosed result | Disclosed ROI | **Implied spend (derived: pipeline ÷ ROI)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 "Fintech Predictions" | ~$80k pipeline | **4.6×** | **≈ $17,400** |
| 2025 "Fintech Predictions" | $382k pipeline, 70 leads | **20.1×** | **≈ $19,000** |

Scope of that spend: **five newsletters** (TLDR Tech, Fintech, AI, Founders, DevOps), i.e.
~2,400 list members across the ecosystem. Plaid's stated context: *"With a small budget to
work with, each placement had to be precise — ideally delivering at least 4.0x ROI."*

**These are DERIVED, not disclosed.** TLDR published no spend figure, and ROI is the
advertiser's own claim. The derivation is simple division and is shown so it can be rejected.

### 5. Display / network CPMs on developer inventory — the floor the direct price must beat

#### 5.1 Carbon Ads (the only dev network with a fully published rate card)

Source: <https://www.carbonads.net/media-kit> and <https://www.carbonads.net/faq>
("Last reviewed: **September 2, 2026**"), retrieved 2026-10-02. Carbon is operated by
BuySellAds and reaches **350+** developer/designer sites.

| Figure | Value | Whose price it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Advertiser pays, Developer audience** | **$6 CPM** | Carbon's own card. *"We don't charge a $40 CPM like ProductHunt, or a $10 CPM like StackOverflow."* |
| Advertiser pays, Creator audience | $5 CPM | Carbon |
| **Publisher earns** | **$1.20–$2.30 CPM** | Carbon's FAQ to publishers |
| Added value available to publisher | up to **+15%** (5% for KPIs, 5% for GA4 access / weekly conversion reporting, 5% for creative refresh every 45–90 days) | Carbon |
| Minimum campaign | **1M impressions/month** recommended, prepayment required | Carbon |
| Click-through, Carbon Classic | 0.8%–2.1% | Carbon |
| Viewability | 95%+; "brand lift 23% avg increase in aided awareness" | Carbon |
| Ad blocking | whitelisted on AdBlock Plus; "typically 20–25% impression lift vs standard display" | Carbon |
| Total paid to publishers since 2010 | **$120M+** | Carbon |
| Publisher admission | **by invitation only**; exclusive network — Carbon only, never backfilled | Carbon |

**The spread is the whole business model: Carbon charges an advertiser $6 CPM and pays the
publisher $1.20–$2.30.** A publisher's realistic display ceiling on a curated dev site is
therefore roughly **$1.20–$3.65 CPM** (top of range plus the full 15% added value).

#### 5.2 AdSense — a share, not a price

[Google's own AdSense revenue-share page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195)
publishes: publishers receive **80%** of AdSense for Content revenue, or **about 68%** when
Google Ads is the buy-side. **Google publishes a revenue share, not an expected eCPM or RPM
for a site of a given traffic size.** There is therefore no official Google CPM expectation
to benchmark against — the network figures in §5.1 are the only usable published dev-inventory
price. Stating this explicitly rather than inventing a Google benchmark.

#### 5.3 The contrast, stated plainly

| | Cost to deliver impressions to a developer |
|---|---|
| Carbon, publisher side | **$1.20–$3.65 CPM** |
| Carbon, advertiser side | **$6 CPM** |
| rnui.dev direct sponsorship at $500/mo and V = 28,600 visitors | **$17.5 CPM** on a total-visitor basis |

A direct-sold sponsorship at rnui.dev's recommended entry price is **4.8× to 14.6× the Carbon
publisher rate and 2.9× the Carbon advertiser rate.** It must therefore be *won* on something
display cannot buy:

1. **Permanence.** A Carbon impression is gone in milliseconds. A catalogue listing is on the
   page forever and in the site's sitemap. (Web Tools Weekly sells exactly this and says so:
   *"All sponsorships include permanent placement in the newsletter's web archive."*)
2. **Persona exactness.** Carbon's audience is 64.6% backend / 26.2% mobile across 350 sites.
   rnui.dev's is ~100% React Native on one site.
3. **Discovery intent.** A developer on rnui.dev is mid-evaluation — the survey in §3.3 shows
   RN developers are overwhelmingly documentation-driven at the moment of choice.
4. **Editorial endorsement.** TWIR sells this explicitly: *"improve your SEO profile with a
   new do-follow backlink"* and *"get a lower Cost-Per-Click."*

**And rnui.dev must never lose this comparison on price.** If the buyer is a solo maintainer or
a 3-person startup (64% of the traffic, per Carbon's team-size table), $6 CPM on Carbon is
~$60/month for the entire network and will look cheap next to a $500 invoice. The ladder
therefore starts at **rung 1, $400/month**, and the *first* pitch to a small vendor should be a
listing, not a package.

### 6. Negotiation mechanics and standard package shapes

Best available primary material is [TWIR's FAQ](https://thisweekinreact.com/sponsor), which
publishes its operating terms unusually fully. These are the norms a seller in this niche
actually operates under:

**Ordering & payment**
- Invoice issued on booking; **payment due within 15 days, before publication**.
- Company details for invoicing collected up front (name, address, VAT/tax number, contact).
- Ad copy due **at least one week before the issue date**; publisher **reviews and validates**
  it. If copy is late, the publisher will reuse a previous ad or write one from the landing
  page.
- **Cancellation is not permitted at the last minute** — the publisher runs the slot anyway.
- Refunds: Native Weekly offers **full refund if not yet published, none once sent**.

**Dates & contracts**
- TWIR: dates are **contractual only if a contract is signed**; otherwise best-effort, with
  practical slippage of about one issue.
- Both properties **book ~3 months ahead**. A published calendar is a real selling tool — TWIR
  publishes its sponsorship calendar publicly and allows a **company alias** for sponsors who
  do not want to be named. (rnui.dev, running PostHog per `CONTEXT.md`, can do the same.)

**Exclusivity**
- The one lever both publishers hand sponsors: **"competitors we do not want to advertise
  alongside."** TWIR's answer is blunt — *"we will do our best to not match with a competitor
  on a best-effort basis based on our intuition, but there will be no guarantee"* unless a
  contract is signed.
- **This is the single most valuable thing rnui.dev can sell that a newsletter cannot: real,
  contractual category exclusivity.** A directory can hold *only one* animation library, *only
  one* crash reporter. That is enforceable, permanent, and directly prevents a sponsor's
  competitor from appearing beside it. Native Weekly can only offer best-effort.

**Reporting & tracking**
- TWIR: **no reporting by default.** On request, raw email-provider stats (open rate, ad
  clicks) one week after the send — and it warns both are unreliable (Apple Mail privacy,
  Gmail clipping, corporate security bots).
- Both publishers recommend the **sponsor tracks its own side**: UTM parameters (publishers
  add defaults if you don't supply your own), landing-page analytics, and your own short-link
  domain (TWDR explicitly asks sponsors not to use bit.ly as it flags as spam).
- Carbon, by contrast, offers up to **15% added value** in exchange for defined KPIs, GA4
  read access or weekly conversion reporting, and creative refreshes — i.e. a network
  *sells* the reporting a direct deal has to give away.

**Standard inclusions to copy**
Placement (logo/banner, copy length limit, number of links) · one sponsor per slot · term
length and issue count · category exclusivity · a do-follow link · permanent archive/sitemap
presence · newsletter mention · a sponsored post · a Discord or community mention · defined
reporting. Every one of these appears on a first-party card except the Discord mention, which
is a directory-native asset rnui.dev can add cheaply.

## How to price this site specifically

### Definitions

- **V** = monthly visitors to rnui.dev (owner-supplied)
- **D** = monthly unique React Native developers reached
- **r** = D ÷ V. For a specialist directory, most visitors *are* the target persona; the tables
  below assume **r = 0.35** as a placeholder and the owner should replace it. **Sensitivity:**
  r scales every cost-per-developer figure linearly, and does not affect CPM-on-V at all.
- Currency **USD** unless a cell says EUR.

### The ladder, with its reasoning

**Rung 1 — Featured catalogue listing. $400–$900/month, billed quarterly.**
Open at **$900/mo ($2,700/quarter)**; expect to settle **$400–600/mo**.
*Why this is the entry rung and is priced below a newsletter slot:* a permanent listing in a
catalogue is not an impression — it is an artefact. It is indexed, it is in the sitemap, it
accrues backlinks, and it is consulted at the moment of library selection (§3.3). The anchor
is TWIR's best price for sustained top-of-newsletter presence on a 40,297-sub list:
**€1,200/send × 8 sends = €9,600 for 8 weeks**. A monthly listing is a longer-running,
lower-frequency asset and should not exceed a quarter of that. Sell **one sponsor per category**
(animation, navigation, backend/data, analytics, monetization, crash & monitoring, auth,
notifications, maps/media) — nine categories means the listing never competes with a sponsor's
competitor, and each is a separate sale.

**Rung 2 — Newsletter placement, top of send. $250–$500 per send.**
*Anchors, both first-party:* Native Weekly's featured placement is **$440/send** on 5,600 subs
at 61% open. TWIR's React-Native link is **€150/send** on 40,297 subs — the RN-specific floor
inside a property 7× larger.
*How to set the number:* if rnui.dev's newsletter is **under ~2,000 subscribers**, price at or
below Native Weekly and expect **$150–$250**. At **3,000–6,000** with an open rate above 50%,
**$350–$440** is defensible and directly comparable to Native Weekly. Above 6,000 with a 55%+ open
rate you can exceed Native Weekly, because you would then have both precision *and* scale — a
combination no property in this niche has. **Always sell the 4-issue bundle, never a single
issue:** TWIR's own recommended offer is 4 issues, and its volume discount runs 20–37.5%.

**Rung 3 — Sponsored post. $500–$1,500.**
Written by the owner, on rnui.dev, linking to the product. *Shape from two first-party/attributed
rate cards:* Web Tools Weekly charges **up to $375** for a paid product review against a $10–15
classified floor and a $200 top-ad combo — a **25–37× spread** between a badge and a written
piece, and a published demonstration that the written piece is the expensive rung. JavaScript
Kicks charges **$29 per sponsored post** as the market's absolute bottom.
*Practical note:* these vendors' content teams will usually want to supply or approve the copy.
Offer that; it lowers your effort and raises their willingness to pay. Do not offer ghostwriting
they can publish elsewhere.

**Rung 4 — Dedicated landing / showcase page. $1,500–$3,000 per quarter.**
**No published rate for this exists anywhere in dev media** — it is an unpublished asset, which
means the owner sets the number alone. Ceiling it at the cost of comparable sustained exposure on
a much larger list: TWIR's 4-issue top-placement bundle is **€5,000 (~$5,430)**, and this package
delivers a permanent artefact *plus* four newsletter placements for less. Include the catalogue
listing for the term.

**Rung 5 — Quarter package ("Founding Sponsor"). $4,000–$8,000 per quarter.**
Exclusive category for the quarter + 4 newsletter placements + 1 sponsored post + 1 showcase page
+ Discord/community credit + a footer "supported by" credit. One such sponsor per category per
quarter, max 3–4 total (matching TLDR's published cap of 3 advertisers per newsletter).
*Ceiling justification:* the closest disclosed analogue is Plaid's TLDR campaign at an implied
**≈$19,000** for five 8M-subscriber newsletters. $8,000/quarter (~$2,700/month) for a 100%
React Native property with contractual exclusivity is a conservative ask against that.

**Rung 6 — Annual. $12,000–$19,000, prepaid, 15–20% below the quarterly rate.**
The discount matches observed first-party volume behaviour (TWIR: −20% from 1→8 issues).

### Effective CPM and cost per developer — the table the owner actually needs

**Rung 1 at the recommended entry price of $500/month.** CPM on V = 500 ÷ V × 1,000.
Cost per unique developer = 500 ÷ D = 500 ÷ (r·V).

| D (monthly unique RN devs) | V at r = 0.35 | **Cost per unique developer** | **Effective CPM on V** | Uplift vs Carbon publisher $1.20–$2.30 | Uplift vs Carbon advertiser $6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 5,700 | **$0.25** | **$87.7** | 38× – 73× | 14.6× |
| 5,000 | 14,300 | $0.10 | $35.0 | 15× – 29× | 5.8× |
| 10,000 | 28,600 | **$0.05** | **$17.5** | 7.6× – 14.6× | 2.9× |
| 25,000 | 71,400 | $0.02 | $7.0 | 3.0× – 5.8× | 1.2× |
| 50,000 | 142,900 | $0.01 | $3.5 | 1.5× – 2.9× | 0.6× |

*(Uplift = the site's effective CPM divided by the network CPM. At 50,000 devs the $500/month
price is **below** Carbon's $6 advertiser CPM — which is the correct and honest signal that at
that scale the listing is cheap and the owner should be charging materially more than the entry
rung.)*

**Same arithmetic at rung 5, $6,000/month:**

| D | V at r = 0.35 | Cost per unique developer | Effective CPM on V | Uplift vs $6 advertiser CPM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 5,700 | **$3.00** | **$1,052** | 175× |
| 5,000 | 14,300 | $1.20 | $420 | 70× |
| 10,000 | 28,600 | $0.60 | $210 | 35× |
| 25,000 | 71,400 | $0.24 | $84 | 14× |
| 50,000 | 142,900 | $0.12 | $42 | 7× |

**Reading these correctly — the whole pricing argument in one paragraph.** Cost per unique
developer is *not* the justification; at $0.05–$3.00 per developer no vendor needs that argument.
The justification is that **Carbon cannot deliver this persona at any price.** Carbon's own
media kit says its audience is 64.6% backend and 26.2% mobile spread across 350 unrelated
sites; a Carbon buy sized to deliver 1,000,000 impressions costs **$6,000** and produces a
non-permanent, non-persona-definite, ad-blockable impression. The equivalent spend at rnui.dev
produces a **permanent, topically-exact, indexable placement** to an audience that is 100% the
buyer's persona, in the one category where the buyer is *actively choosing a vendor*. So the
correct objection-handling line is never "it is cheap per developer" — it is **"no network can
buy this audience, because no network has it."**

**The counter-pressure, from first-party data, which the owner must state out loud.**
64% of developer traffic comes from teams of 1–50 people (Carbon). At 1–10 person companies,
budgets are personal. A $900/month listing is a real ask to a solo maintainer and an
unaffordable one to many. This is why rung 1 must exist at **$400/month**, why the ladder
ascends rather than starting high, and why a **first placement should be discounted** to buy
a case study — the standard play, and the one both TWIR and Native Weekly structurally enable
by selling multi-issue bundles that prove out over a term.

**Sanity check against the ecosystem rnui.dev sits in.** React Native Directory reports
**14,012,472,620 cumulative monthly downloads** across 2,715 packages. That flow is the
monetisable surface, and rnui.dev is a peer of it — not a newsletter bolted onto it. A
sponsor's media value is best measured as *"placed inside the channel where 14 billion monthly
npm downloads are decided"*, and priced accordingly, rather than as an email impression.
